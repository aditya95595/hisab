Percentages without the headache

Nearly every percentage question is one of these three shapes. "X% of Y" comes up in discounts and marks; "X is what % of Y" in scores and shares; "percent change" in prices, salaries and growth.

The classic trap is percentage points vs percent: a rise from 10% to 15% is 5 percentage points, but a 50% relative increase. Keep them straight and the numbers stay honest.

Percentage questions, answered

How do I find X percent of Y?

Multiply X by Y and divide by 100. For example, 15% of 200 is (15 × 200) ÷ 100 = 30.

How do I find what percent X is of Y?

Divide X by Y and multiply by 100. For example, 30 is (30 ÷ 200) × 100 = 15% of 200.

What is the difference between percent and percentage points?

A move from 10% to 15% is a 5-percentage-point rise, but a 50% relative increase. Mixing the two is one of the most common percentage mistakes.
